Draft one complete issue before buying your software

Bottom line: Write a sample issue, choose a publishing routine, and price the work. Then use that issue to test whether a platform fits your publication.

A blank newsletter template leaves too many decisions open. Start with a named place and a useful promise, such as a weekend events digest for a particular town. Write a sample issue using information you can verify directly.

Give the first issue a source and an owner for each item

This is our suggested issue outline. Adapt the number of items to the reporting time available. Every factual detail needs a source that can settle it, such as an organizer's current event listing.

For a paid business feature that needs a disclosure, the FTC's native-ad guidance calls for a clear and prominent label. [3] Put the label next to the item it explains.

Test authentication and signup with a real sample email

Google requires SPF or DKIM for senders to personal Gmail accounts. [1] Ask the email provider to demonstrate the setup for your sending domain and check a delivered sample.

Google recommends opting in and confirming each recipient's email address. [1] Our suggested signup test is to subscribe with an address you control, confirm it, receive an issue, and try unsubscribing.

The FTC's commercial-email guide bases the classification on the message's primary purpose. [2] Keep that distinction in the publishing checklist when an issue carries offers or promotions.

For commercial email, the FTC guide requires a valid postal address and an explanation of how to stop future marketing messages. [2] Test those elements in the delivered email.

Requirements change: This is a U.S. planning guide, not a complete compliance checklist. Recheck Google sender guidelines and FTC commercial-email guidance for the message and audience you intend to serve.

Count reporting time alongside the cash expenses

Our planning method separates recurring cash costs from the time spent reporting and publishing. Add a labor allowance if you want the cost target to include your work. Enter setup costs separately and choose how many months to spread them across.

The worksheet's budget section shows the formula and the inputs. A sponsorship price there is an assumption you enter. The result is the number of paid placements needed to cover the modeled cost, without a prediction that those placements will sell.

Give a prospective sponsor a concrete placement to evaluate: where it appears, what it contains, and when it runs. Keep any audience numbers tied to the publication's actual records.
